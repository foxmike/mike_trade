# BUY backlog C — VWAP and intraday methods

> **Review note:** this batch record was written before the independent review. Counts here are branch-local (pre-integration); the reviewed behaviour, corrections and final 182-strategy counts are in `docs/BUY_SELL_BACKLOG_CD_REVIEW.md`, which takes precedence.

Three of the six candidates produce distinct `1.0.0` BUY entries; three map to existing registered strategies. No source below is claimed to prescribe Mike_Trade's numeric defaults or to establish profitability. Every new signal uses completed M1 candles, a fresh regular-session context, `WATCHING_BUY` eligibility, conservative gap handling, a same-bar replay guard, and no order or portfolio logic.

The morning impulse–pullback–resumption reading selected here is exactly the new time-bounded `first_pullback_buy`, not a fourth method. |

The mean-reversion mapping is not `bollinger_band_mean_reversion`: Bollinger uses an unweighted rolling close distribution, while the registered VWAP bands use cumulative session volume weights. The continuation mapping is not `vwap_bounce`, which only needs an upward central-line crossing, nor `ema_pullback`, which tests a fast EMA. `first_pullback_buy` differs from `bull_flag_breakout` by consuming only the first qualifying morning impulse/correction, enforcing tapering and renewed volume, and permitting a close through corrective highs below the impulse peak; a bull flag is a reusable pole-and-flag price pattern. `momentum_breakout` instead tests a rolling high with volume and needs no preceding correction. These distinctions are rule-level; overlapping signals on some candles remain possible.

## Published concepts versus engineering rules

The [Schwab VWAP discussion](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/how-to-use-volume-weighted-indicators-trading) treats daily VWAP and its deviation bands as intraday trend, support/resistance, and oversold references; it notes that a dip below VWAP followed by recovery can signal renewed buying. [StockCharts' candlesticks-and-support guide](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/candlestick-charts/candlesticks-and-support) describes bullish candles at support. Neither prescribes `vwap_rejection`'s same-candle condition, prior wholly-above test, or 0.5% penetration limit: these are disclosed engineering choices. This method is not `vwap_bounce` (prior *close* must be at/below VWAP), not `vwap_pullback` (a separate setup and later confirmation candle), and not a lower-deviation-band reversion. `vwap_rejection` uses the production typical-price VWAP formula `Σ(volume×(high+low+close)/3)/Σvolume` through `session_vwap_series`, checks the recomputed latest/prior values against the shared context VWAP, and requires complete-from-open session history. Parameter: `max_penetration_pct=0.5` (0.1–5). Minimum/default warmup is 3 bars: the session-open candle itself cannot trade wholly above its own VWAP, so a prior wholly-above bar needs at least one earlier candle. State: session ID and last signal bar; no recursive calculation state. If the supplied history cannot reproduce the shared VWAP, it fails closed.

[StockCharts' anchored VWAP definition](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-overlays/anchored-vwap) permits a significant low as the anchor and uses the ordinary volume-weighted typical-price formula from that event bar onward. The exact choice of the *first confirmed* strict swing low and a close-cross entry are engineering choices. With `strength=2` (1–10), the pivot is only known after two completed right-hand bars. The pivot bar starts the calculation, but neither it nor its confirmation bar can trigger an entry. There is no re-anchoring to a later low. `VWAPDeviationBandsCalculator` at multiplier zero supplies the production-matched VWAP and a JSON-safe cumulative snapshot; the strategy also persists the anchor timestamp, last processed bar, previous close/VWAP, and session. Its dynamic history requirement is `2×strength+2` (default 6). Session change starts fresh; missing bars, zero volume, or loss of both snapshot and complete session history fail closed. The anchor is a within-session event, not a disguised session-open VWAP; it is not automatically a forecast of support.

[Warrior Trading's first-pullback description](https://www.warriortrading.com/can-you-trade-in-a-cash-account/) calls for a strong-volume initial surge, the first orderly pullback, and an entry as a candle makes a new high. Its [pullback discussion](https://www.warriortrading.com/pull-back-trading-strategy/) describes brief, shallower, lower-volume corrections and renewed breakout volume. The code's deterministic interpretation uses `impulse_bars=3` (3–10) consecutive higher highs, higher lows and closes; an `impulse_pct=1%` (0.1–10%) close gain over the first bar's open; final impulse volume at least `volume_multiple=1.2` (1–5) times the average of its predecessor bars; then the **first** lower-close corrective bar. The correction must remain above the impulse low, retrace no more than `max_retrace=0.5` (0.1–0.9) of impulse range, last at most `max_pullback_bars=5` (1–15) bars, and have volume below the impulse mean. Entry is the first completed close above the highest corrective high with volume at least `volume_multiple` times corrective average, before `entry_end_minute=90` (15–180) after 09:30 ET. These thresholds, a close instead of an intrabar stop entry, and the time cutoff are engineering defaults, not published universal values. Dynamic history is `impulse_bars+2` (default 5). The JSON-safe setup phase and prices/volumes persist through restart and ineligible intervals; deep, long, or high-volume first corrections consume the setup for the session. No EMA/VWAP touch is required, so this is not an alias of either pullback strategy. This price/volume-only implementation does not reproduce discretionary catalyst, float, news, or MACD filters in some descriptions of first-pullback trading.

All three strategies use the exact NYSE regular-session open and require a contiguous from-open prefix when initially building state. The two stateful rules can continue from their persisted snapshots with a shorter subsequent history, provided every newly processed M1 bar is present. Latest-bar gaps or a corrupt/truncated state without a complete prefix produce `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`; same-bar replays do not trade twice. The regular-session gate controls whether a BUY signal may be emitted while state advancement continues when BUY is temporarily ineligible. SHADOW and BACKTEST use the existing production/research registrations; sizing, stops, market-state authorization and fills remain engine-owned.
