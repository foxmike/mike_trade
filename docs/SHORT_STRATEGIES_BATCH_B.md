# SHORT strategies — Batch B

These ten `1.0.0` strategies open short positions; they never issue long exits. All use completed, consecutive M1 candles, require a fresh regular-session evaluation context and the engine's `WATCHING_SHORT` eligibility, reject gaps across their calculation window, and suppress a second signal on the same candle. The shared SHORT gate and `last_signal_bar` state handle these rules. The context, not each historical candle, supplies session eligibility; if an input feed supplies contiguous premarket candles, they may be part of a lookback crossing into the regular session. All calculations are finite-window, so there is no recursive indicator snapshot to persist; the signal-bar key is JSON-safe and survives restart. Ineligible strategies have no recursive state to advance. History requirements below are the dynamic `required_bars` values; the default is also each spec's `warmup_bars`. A qualifying close may gap through a level, but an unclosed bar cannot signal.

The SELL/BULL references are methodology starting points, not execution instructions. SELL closes a long; these rules only request a SHORT entry. Structural pivots use the shared `swing_pivots` strict fractal convention: a pivot requires `strength` completed bars on each side (two for fixed five-bar pivots), and becomes observable only on the last confirming bar. There is no retrospective trade on its center candle.

| Strategy | Exact trigger and confirmation | Defaults; required history | Reference and intentional differences / limitations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `short_bearish_engulfing` | Prior completed bullish real body is engulfed by the latest bearish real body (`indicators.finite.engulfing`); the pre-pattern close advanced over `trend_bars`. | `trend_bars=3` (2–50); `trend_bars+2` (5). | `sell/bearish_engulfing_exit.py`; StockCharts ChartSchool, *Candlestick Bearish Reversal Patterns*. Adds an explicit prior advance; no mandatory gap or volume test. |
| `short_shooting_star_reversal` | A shooting star after a prior close-to-close rise is followed by the *next* completed close strictly below the star low. Star geometry uses `is_shooting_star`: upper shadow ≥ `shadow_ratio` × real body and lower shadow ≤ `max_lower_shadow` × range. | `trend_bars=5` (2–50), `shadow_ratio=2` (1–10), `max_lower_shadow=0.1` (0–0.5); `trend_bars+3` (8). | `sell/shooting_star_reversal.py`; Steve Nison, *Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques*; StockCharts ChartSchool shooting star. The star itself cannot emit an entry. No opening-gap requirement. |
| `short_double_top_breakdown` | Latest two confirmed swing highs have relative difference ≤ `peak_tolerance`; a **confirmed** low between them is at least `min_decline` below the lower peak; the completed close crosses from at/above to below this trough support. First peak must lead prior highs; no subsequent high may invalidate the peaks. | `strength=2` (1–10), `lookback=60` (15–300), `peak_tolerance=0.03` (0–0.10), `min_decline=0.10` (0.01–0.50); `lookback+1` (61), with `lookback≥4×strength+3`. | `sell/double_top_reversal.py`; StockCharts ChartSchool, *Double Top Reversal*. Requires a confirmed trough, not an unconfirmed minimum. Daily-chart percentage thresholds are mechanically applied to M1; no volume filter. |
| `short_head_and_shoulders_breakdown` | Four latest confirmed highs form rising predecessor, left shoulder, higher head and lower right shoulder. Confirmed intervening reaction lows define a linearly projected neckline. Previous close is at/above its neckline and current completed close below its own projected neckline; no post-shoulder high reaches the head. | `strength=2` (1–10), `lookback=60` (20–300); `lookback+1` (61), with `lookback≥6×strength+1`. | `sell/head_and_shoulders_breakdown.py`; StockCharts ChartSchool, *Head and Shoulders Top*. Neckline lows must themselves be confirmed. Shoulder symmetry and volume are not enforced. |
| `short_trendline_breakdown` | Two latest confirmed five-bar swing lows rise. Project their straight line to the previous and latest bar; close crosses from at/above its prior projection to strictly below its current projection. | `lookback=30` (8–200); `lookback+1` (31). | `sell/trendline_breakdown.py`; John J. Murphy, *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets*, trendlines; Bill Williams five-bar fractals. A falling/flat line is ineligible. |
| `short_previous_swing_low_break` | Close crosses from at/above to below the latest **confirmed five-bar pivot low** in the prior lookback. | `lookback=20` (5–100); `lookback+1` (21). | `sell/previous_swing_low_break.py`; Bill Williams, *Trading Chaos*, fractal support. This structural pivot is distinct from a rolling Donchian channel low and cannot use the current bar as support. |
| `short_lower_low_confirmation` | The low centered two bars before the latest completed candle *newly* qualifies as a five-bar fractal and is strictly below the preceding confirmed pivot low. The signal timestamp is the latest confirmation bar. | `lookback=30` (7–200); `lookback+1` (31). | `sell/lower_low_confirmation.py`; Dow lower-low trend structure; Bill Williams five-bar fractals. Does not fire on every subsequent candle below the old low. |
| `short_failed_breakout_reversal` | Prior close strictly exceeded the highest high of the `period` bars **immediately before** that breakout bar; latest completed close falls strictly below that same level. | `period=20` (2–200); `period+2` (22). | `sell/failed_breakout_exit.py`; Thomas Bulkowski, *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns*, failed breakouts; prior-period Donchian convention. Corrects the SELL reference's one-bar-shifted reference window and excludes the breakout bar. Failure must occur on the next bar; equality is not failure. |
| `short_bear_flag_breakdown` | Within the prior `lookback` bars, locate the lowest pole low. At least two observed bars precede it within `pole_bars`, with a declining close and a high-to-low pole drop ≥ `pole_pct` of pole high. The following `min_flag_bars…max_flag_bars` corrective bars rise in closing price, keep all lows above the pole low, and retrace no more than `max_retrace` of pole height. Latest completed close is strictly below the flag's lowest low. | `lookback=60` (20–300), `pole_bars=15` (3–100), `pole_pct=1%` (0.1–30%), `min_flag_bars=3` (2–50), `max_flag_bars=20` (3–100), `max_retrace=0.5` (0.1–0.9); `lookback+1` (61). Also require min≤max and lookback>max+1. | `buy/bull_flag_breakout.py` mirrored where justified; Thomas Bulkowski, *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns*, flags; John J. Murphy, *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets*, chapter 7. Explicit price/length thresholds are implementation choices, not source-prescribed M1 profitability levels. No volume filter, target, or subjective visual classification. |
| `short_bearish_momentum_divergence` | The just-confirmed price swing high exceeds the preceding confirmed high while close-difference momentum `close[pivot]−close[pivot−momentum_period]` is lower at the matching second pivot. Signal occurs only when the latest bar confirms the second pivot. | `momentum_period=10` (2–100), `strength=2` (1–10), `lookback=50` (10–300); `momentum_period+lookback` (60), with `lookback≥4×strength+2`. | `sell/bearish_momentum_divergence.py`; John J. Murphy, *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets*, momentum divergence; Bill Williams confirmed fractals. No arbitrary pairing with separate oscillator peaks or later bars. |

All ten are registered through `app.build_runtime` and `research.commands._research_registry`; per-symbol SHORT collections, SHADOW and BACKTEST consume those existing registries. These are deterministic pattern definitions, not claims of predictive accuracy. M1 adaptations of classical patterns can be noisy; backtest and live execution may differ due to fills, latency, borrow availability, margin and other engine-owned constraints. No strategy submits an order or manages a position.
